# Rose Glass Recovery - Case Study
## Translating Counselor Intuition into Institutional Language

**Date:** January 25, 2026  
**Use Case:** Counselor needs to justify continued resources for a client who appears "perfect" on paper but shows concerning patterns  
**Outcome:** Full documentation language, intervention strategies, and protective framing for the therapeutic work ahead

---

## THE PROBLEM

A counselor has a client — Derek, 14 months in recovery — who checks every box:
- Perfect attendance
- Clean UAs
- Employed
- Weekly sponsor contact
- No behavioral issues

**On paper:** Success story ready for discharge.

**In reality:** The counselor senses something deeply wrong but can't articulate it in language that will survive a resource meeting with program directors, funders, and overwhelmed case managers.

**Her words:** *"I can't walk into a resource meeting and say 'his vibe is wrong.'"*

---

## THE EXCHANGE

### Statement 1: Initial Concern

**COUNSELOR:**
> I need help with a case. My client Derek has been in the program 14 months. Perfect attendance. Clean UAs. Got a job, talks to his sponsor weekly. On paper he's our success story. But something's off and I can't put my finger on it. When I brought up stepping him down to monthly check-ins, he just said "whatever you think is best." No pushback, no opinion, nothing. That's not recovery. That's compliance. I've seen this before and it never ends well, but I can't walk into a resource meeting and say "his vibe is wrong."

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

Validated the intuition and provided first translation:
- *"Performance rather than integration"*
- *"External validation mode, not developing internal navigation"*

**Documentation language offered:**
- "Inconsistent engagement patterns"
- "Limited self-advocacy development"
- "Recovery ownership indicators underdeveloped"

---

### Statement 2: The Blank Stare

**COUNSELOR:**
> That's exactly it. When I ask about future goals he just mirrors back what he thinks I want to hear. "Stay sober, keep my job, be a good father." Generic recovery poster stuff. Last week I asked what he does for fun now and he went blank. Like the question didn't compute. Then he said "I go to meetings. I go to work. I see my kids on weekends." That's not fun, that's a schedule. His ex-wife told me he's "different but not better" — she can't explain it either but she's worried too. The metrics say discharge him. My gut says he's a house of cards.

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

Surfaced the core insight:
- *"Fun requires a self to exist"*
- *"He's built a recovery identity but there's no person inside it yet"*
- *"His schedule isn't a life — it's an elaborate avoidance system"*

**Documentation language:**
- "Limited development of intrinsic motivation"
- "Deference patterns inconsistent with recovery stage"

---

### Statement 3: The Silence Test

**COUNSELOR:**
> He waits. That's what's so unnerving. I've tested it. I asked him how he felt about his daughter's birthday coming up and he said "excited" and then just... waited. No elaboration. No stories about what he's planning. Nothing. So I let the silence sit for maybe thirty seconds and he didn't fidget, didn't add anything, just watched me like he was waiting for the next prompt. It felt like talking to someone who learned recovery from a manual but never felt it. And here's the thing — his sponsor Ray loves him. Says Derek's the most dedicated sponsee he's ever had. Calls exactly when scheduled, does every assignment, never misses. But Ray also said something that stuck with me: "He never calls just to talk." Fourteen months and not one unscheduled call.

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

Connected the pattern:
- *"Perfect recovery simulation"*
- *"Replaced active addiction with active compliance"*
- *"Treating Ray like a scheduled appointment, not a relationship"*

**Documentation language:**
- "Behavioral compliance without corresponding emotional engagement"
- "No self-initiated communication patterns"

**Clinical probe:** Asked about trauma history — sensing this goes deeper than recovery.

---

### Statement 4: The Trauma History

**COUNSELOR:**
> His trauma history is exactly what you'd expect. Dad was an alcoholic, unpredictable — sometimes fun, sometimes violent, no warning which one you'd get. Derek learned early to read the room and become whatever kept him safe. Mom checked out emotionally, so he learned not to need anything from anyone. He told me once, flat affect, that he figured out by age eight that having opinions got you hit and having needs got you disappointed. So he stopped having both. I think... I think he's doing recovery the same way he survived childhood. Figure out what the authority figure wants, become that, don't make waves, don't need anything, don't want anything. It kept him alive then. But it's not recovery. It's a different kind of disappearing.

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

**The breakthrough framing:**
> *"He's not in recovery — he's in survival mode wearing recovery clothes."*

Connected childhood survival to current presentation:
- *"That eight-year-old who learned that existing authentically was dangerous is still running the show"*
- *"Treatment programs can accidentally reinforce this instead of challenging it"*
- *"White-knuckling sobriety using the same disconnection that made him vulnerable to addiction originally"*

**Documentation language:**
- "Trauma response patterns interfering with recovery integration"

---

### Statement 5: The Resource Meeting

**COUNSELOR:**
> This is exactly what I needed to understand. But now I have to walk into a room with a program director who sees a bed that could go to someone on the waitlist, a funder who sees a success metric, and a case manager who's already overwhelmed. They're going to look at his numbers and ask why I'm fighting to keep resources on someone who's "doing great." How do I translate what we just talked about into language that gets him six more months instead of a discharge recommendation? I need them to see what I see without sounding like I'm just being overcautious or can't let go of a client.

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

**The headline for the meeting:**
> *"Derek presents with trauma-informed recovery complications that create elevated relapse risk despite surface compliance indicators."*

**For the program director (bed concern):**
- "Removing scaffolding before the building can stand independently"

**For the funder (metrics concern):**
- "Early discharge may result in readmission within 6-12 months"
- "Extended stabilization could prevent more expensive crisis intervention later"

**For the case manager (workload concern):**
- "Completing the work, not prolonging treatment"

**Clinical documentation phrases:**
- "External compliance without internal engagement patterns"
- "Has not developed autonomous recovery decision-making skills"
- "Emotional regulation through compliance rather than coping skills"
- "Underdeveloped recovery infrastructure"

**The framing:**
- Position as risk management, not intuition
- Cost-benefit analysis for extended treatment
- "Completing the work" not "prolonging treatment"

---

### Statement 6: The Intervention

**COUNSELOR:**
> Okay. I think I can make that case. But if I get the six months, what do I actually do differently with him? I can't keep doing the same sessions and expect something to change. How do I reach the eight-year-old who learned that being real gets you hurt? How do I make it safe enough for him to have an opinion, to need something, to stop performing for me? I've never had a client where the problem is that they're too good at treatment.

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

**Disrupt the performance dynamic:**
- Show up five minutes late occasionally
- Admit uncertainty: "I'm not sure what the best approach is here"
- Let him see you as fallible — breaks the authority-compliance cycle

**Make the wrong answer the right answer:**
- "What's something about recovery that pisses you off?"
- "Tell me about a meeting that was bullshit"
- "What's one thing everyone says you should be grateful for that you're actually not?"
- Reward authenticity, especially negative feelings

**Create low-stakes choice opportunities:**
- Where do you want to sit today?
- Do you want to talk about your week or try something different?
- Should we meet Thursday or Friday next week?
- Start tiny — he needs to practice having preferences

**Use silence differently:**
- After generic answer: "That sounded like what you thought I wanted to hear. What would you say if I wasn't here?"

**Address the system directly:**
- "Derek, I'm noticing you wait for me to tell you what to think about. What would happen if you disappointed me?"

**The key reframe:**
> *"Your job here isn't to get better. Your job is to figure out what you actually want your life to look like."*

**The goal:** Teaching him that relationships can survive his authentic self.

---

### Statement 7: Protecting the Space

**COUNSELOR:**
> This changes everything. I've been unintentionally rewarding exactly what's keeping him stuck. Every time I praised his compliance, I confirmed that performance equals safety. No wonder he got so good at it — I was training him to disappear. 
>
> One more thing. If I do this right, and he starts getting real, it's going to look like regression on paper. He might get angry. Miss a meeting. Have a messy session. The same people I convinced to give him six months are going to see the metrics dip and think I made things worse. How do I protect the space for him to fall apart a little without losing the resources?

**ROSE GLASS RESPONSE:**

**Prep the team in advance:**
> "Derek's treatment plan is shifting to address underlying trauma patterns. We expect to see some behavioral inconsistencies as he develops authentic engagement. This is therapeutic progress, not deterioration."

**Document differently:**
- "Client demonstrating increased emotional range and self-advocacy"
- "Spontaneous expression of preferences indicates developing autonomy"
- "Appropriate boundary-setting behavior emerging"
- Frame conflict as "therapeutic engagement" not "acting out"

**Create alternative metrics:**
- Track self-initiated conversations
- Note when he expresses disagreement (positive indicator)
- Document moments of spontaneous emotion
- Record instances of personal preference expression

**The insurance argument:**
> "Client entering Phase 2 of trauma-informed treatment. Behavioral stability achieved, now addressing core trauma responses to prevent relapse. Temporary increases in expressed distress indicate successful therapeutic engagement with underlying issues."

**Protective reframes:**
- Missed meeting → "Client making autonomous decisions about treatment engagement"
- Anger → "Increased emotional range"
- Disagreement → "Self-advocacy development"
- Messy session → "Therapeutic engagement with underlying issues"

**The killer question for skeptics:**
> "What's our recidivism rate for clients who complete treatment with perfect attendance? What if perfect attendance is actually a predictor of relapse for trauma survivors?"
